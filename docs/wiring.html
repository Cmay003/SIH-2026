Module notes

  • SX1278 LoRa (Ra-02): 3.3 V ONLY - 5 V destroys it. Fit the antenna before powering.
  • DHT22 temp/humidity: Bare sensor: 10k pull-up DATA->3V3 (modules have one).
  • HC-SR04 water level: 5 V module: ECHO must go through a divider.
  • IR flame sensor: Power it from 3V3 so DO is a 3.3 V signal.
  • MQ135 gas: Heater ~150 mA from 5 V, always on (no deep sleep).
  • Rain gauge (reed switch): Switch between the pin and GND.
  • Capacitive soil moisture v1.2: At 3.3 V supply AO stays below ~3 V.
  • MPU6050 tilt/vibration: I2C address 0x68 (AD0 to GND).
  • PMS5003 particulate: Fan needs 5 V; its UART is 3.3 V logic.
  • pH board (PH-4502C): Calibrate with pH 7 and pH 4 buffers ('p' command).
  • Turbidity (SEN0189): Output 0-4.5 V.
  • 18650 battery sense: Divider from battery + to the pin (high values: tiny drain).
  • SOS push-button: For people without a phone: hold 2 s = SOS at this node's position. Normally-open weatherproof button between the pin and GND; the ESP32's internal pull-up, so no resistor to 3V3. The 1k in series protects the pin from ESD on an outdoor cable. Wakes a deep-sleeping node.
  • Village siren + strobe (12 V): Sounds on a server command (officer / confirmed CRITICAL evacuation hazard), or by itself only when the gateway has been silent 900 s and the water level or gas is at its siren danger level; 180 s per trigger. Switched on the LOW side by a logic-level N-MOSFET (Rds(on) specified at a gate voltage of 3.3 V or less - check its datasheet) or a relay board with an active-HIGH input that works from 3.3 V: drain to the siren / strobe (-), their (+) to the 12 V supply, source to the common GND. 100R gate resistor; 10k gate-to-GND REQUIRED (keeps the siren off while the ESP32 resets). A diode across any coil or motor load (cathode to +12 V). Never an active-LOW relay board.
  • Status LED: On-board LED on most DevKits.

Power budget - typical datasheet values and project estimates, to be MEASURED

  • MQ135 heater: heater resistance 33 ohm +-5 %, heating consumption less than 800 mW at 5 V, so ~150 mA (5 V / 33 ohm) [Hanwei MQ-135 technical data, copy], continuously - this is why a gas node cannot deep-sleep. MQ135_DUTY_CYCLE (config.h) switches it on only around the reports that carry gas: ESTIMATE ~1,190 instead of ~3,640 mAh/day at 5 V (clean air; nothing is saved while the node is elevated), but a leak between wakes is seen up to one period late, it needs a free GPIO for the MOSFET, and it is refused on a siren node (gas is an offline-siren trigger). The datasheet gives no re-heating time after a short off-time - MEASURE.
  • PMS5003: active current max 100 mA, standby max 200 µA; stable data at least 30 s after a wake from sleep (fan) [Plantower PMS5003 datasheet PTQ3004-2015 V1.0 (2019-07-31), copy]. Runs continuously (~2,400 mAh/day at 5 V at the maximum); PMS5003_DUTY_CYCLE (config.h) sleeps it between reports by its serial command - no extra wire - ESTIMATE ~375 mAh/day.
  • Panel size is a design input, not a fact. The project's own estimate (planning/progress.txt): an always-on gas node draws ~200 mA, ~4.8 Ah/day, so one 18650 lasts < 1 day and solar needs roughly a 6 W panel. Measure the node's real current with a USB meter, then size panel and cell for the worst month's sun at the site. The TP4056 (below) caps this: a bigger panel cannot push more than its set current, so (derived, to be MEASURED) the charge into the cell per day is at most about (charge current - load current) x hours of usable sun - e.g. (1 A - 0.2 A) x h must cover 0.2 A x the remaining (24 - h) hours, so h >= ~4.8 h of full-current charging, more after the boost loss. Check that against the measured daily consumption before buying a bigger panel.
  • TP4056: charges the cell to 4.2 V; R_PROG 1.2k = 1 A (950-1050 mA); ends the charge when the current falls to 1/10 of the set value; input (VCC) operating range 4.0-8.0 V, absolute maximum 8 V - check the panel's open-circuit voltage (higher than its working voltage, and highest on cold mornings) against that limit. [TP4056 datasheet (NanJing Top Power ASIC), copy]
  • TP4056 module without load sharing (the common one): OUT+ is wired straight to B+ (the DW01 / FS8205 protection switches the − side, between B− and OUT−), so the load always runs from the cell node, also while charging - the charger's current is split between cell and load. That is fine for this design. Side effect: the charger measures its total output, so a load above 1/10 of the set current (100 mA at 1 A) can keep it from ever seeing the end-of-charge current; it then holds the cell at 4.2 V.
  • MT3608 (VIN = 5 V, 25 °C): quiescent 100 µA typ / 200 µA max when not switching, 1.6 mA typ / 2.2 mA max when switching; 0.1 µA only with EN at 0 V - if your module ties EN to VIN (check the board), it never shuts down. Input 2-24 V. A boost converter draws MORE current from the cell than it delivers at 5 V (input power = output power / efficiency) - measure on the cell side. [MT3608 datasheet Rev 1.1 (Aerosemi), copy]
  • Board regulator: many DevKit boards use an AMS1117-3.3 (Espressif's DevKitC guide does not name the part - read the marking on yours). AMS1117: current limit 900 mA min / 1.1 A typ; quiescent current 5 mA typ / 11 mA max (at VIN-VOUT = 1.5 V); dropout up to 1.3 V at 0.8 A, so it needs ~4.6 V in - that is why the cell (3.0-4.2 V) goes through the boost to 5 V instead of straight to VIN. Its quiescent current flows whenever the board is powered, deep sleep included. [AMS1117 datasheet (Advanced Monolithic Systems), copy]
  • Deep sleep on a dev board: the ESP32 chip itself needs ~10 µA (RTC timer + RTC memory) [ESP32 Series Datasheet v5.3, Table 4-2 (Espressif)], but the board's LDO (above), its USB-UART bridge chip and the 5 V power-on LED [ESP32-DevKitC user guide (Espressif)] keep drawing current while the chip sleeps - MEASURE the sleep current; long runs need a bare module and a low-quiescent regulator (as config.h says).
  • 3.3 V pin budget: the ESP32 and every 3V3 module (SX1278 LoRa (Ra-02), DHT22 temp/humidity, IR flame sensor, Capacitive soil moisture v1.2, MPU6050 tilt/vibration) share the board regulator's current limit - add up their datasheet currents (radio transmit included). The 3V3 pin must not feed 5 V modules or the Village siren + strobe (12 V).
  • Village siren + strobe: its own 12 V supply (battery or adapter) sized for the siren and strobe current from their datasheets - not the node's 18650 / MT3608 chain. Common GND with the ESP32, because the MOSFET switches the (−) side. Take the switch's current rating and the wire gauge from the siren's current (not verified here).
  • One supply at a time: Espressif - the DevKitC must be powered from one and only one of USB, the 5V pin or the 3V3 pin, otherwise the board and/or the supply can be damaged. Disconnect the MT3608 before plugging in USB for Serial / flashing. [ESP32-DevKitC user guide (Espressif)]
